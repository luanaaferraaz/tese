Based on the provided algorithm and properties, here is my analysis of them.

RB-Validity - Satisfied
The broadcaster sends <type0, m> to its neighbours and then receives at least one <type1, m> from a neighbouring process. This property is satisfied because the broadcaster only broadcasts messages when it has not sent an initial message for that particular message (<type0, m>). It does not send <type1, m> in response to received <type0, m> or on its own initiative.

RB-Agreement - Satisfied
If a correct process delivers message m, then it receives at least f + 1 <type1, m> messages from different processes. The property is satisfied because the algorithm states that if a correct process delivers m, and it received f+1 <type1, m> messages, this means that there are at least two distinct processes supporting the delivery of m by sending <type1, m> to their neighbours.

RB-Integrity - Satisfied
A correct process delivers message m only once because of the delivered set. The property is satisfied because a broadcasted message (<type0, m>) can be confirmed by at least one other correct process and hence it cannot force delivery more than once, even if a Byzantine process is faulty or maliciously manipulates the network.
