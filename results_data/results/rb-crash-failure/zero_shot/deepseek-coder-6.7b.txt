Here is the analysis of each property in the context of this algorithm:

1. RB-Validity: The broadcaster (process 0) sends <type0, m> to itself and delivers it if it receives a copy from itself. This ensures that all correct processes eventually receive m through reliable channels. However, there is no guarantee that the process receiving m will forward it to its neighbours.
   - Satisfied

2. RB-Agreement: If one correct process delivers <type0, m>, then it received and forwards <type1, m> to all other processes including itself. Therefore, if any of the non-crashed neighbour processes deliver <type1, m>, that means every correct process eventually receives m from a consistent process (i.e., one that delivers at least once).
   - Satisfied

3. RB-Integrity: The delivered set prevents duplicate delivery. It ensures that no message is delivered more than once. However, the algorithm does not guarantee that if a correct process delivers <type1, m> to its neighbours, it will send another <type0, m> to itself before receiving another copy from its neighbours. So there can be an inconsistency between sent and received messages.
   - Satisfied
